## Introduction

Industrial training bridges the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical
application in engineering education. As part of the B.E. (EEE) curriculum at PSG
College of Technology, the author underwent a one-week industrial training at the
230/110 kV Substation, Samayanallur, operated by the Tamil Nadu Transmission
Corporation Limited (TANTRANSCO), from 22–26 June 2026.

The training covered the construction, operation, and maintenance of major substation
equipment — power transformers, circuit breakers, isolators, CTs, PTs, lightning
arresters, busbars, and protection relays — along with switching operations, safety
practices, and protection schemes used in high-voltage substations.

## About TANTRANSCO

TANTRANSCO is a wholly owned subsidiary of TANGEDCO, established on 1 November 2010
after the restructuring of the former Tamil Nadu Electricity Board (TNEB). It is
responsible for transmitting electrical power across Tamil Nadu, operating an
extensive network of 765 kV, 400 kV, 230 kV, 110 kV, and 66 kV transmission lines
and substations, supported by SCADA systems, digital protection relays, and remote
monitoring/automation.

## About the 230/110 kV Substation (Samayanallur)

The substation steps down power from 230 kV to 110 kV and distributes it to load
centers. Key features:

- **Incoming 230 kV lines:** Alagar Kovil, Pasumalai, Kinnimangalam
- **230 kV double busbar arrangement** with bus coupler for operational flexibility
- **Power transformers:** Two 100 MVA, 230/110 kV units
- **Outgoing 110 kV feeders:** Pasumalai, Arasadi, Sembatty, Manikampatty, Vadipatti,
  Alagar Kovil
- **Auxiliary supply:** Two 11 kV station service transformers
- **DC systems:** 220 V DC and 48 V DC battery banks for protection, control, and
  communication during AC supply failures
- **Relay & Control Room:** Houses protection relays, control/synchronizing panels,
  and SCADA monitoring equipment

## Switchyard Observation

Each incoming feeder passes through a CT, PT, circuit breaker, and isolator before
connecting to the 230 kV double busbar. Power is stepped down via the two 100 MVA
transformers and distributed through the 110 kV outgoing feeders listed above.

## Power Transformer Observation

The two 100 MVA, 230/110 kV oil-immersed transformers use transformer oil as both an
insulating medium and a cooling agent. Heated oil rises and circulates through
radiators to dissipate heat. A conservator tank accommodates oil expansion/contraction,
and a silica gel breather absorbs moisture from incoming air — turning pink when it
needs replacement or regeneration.

## Station Auxiliary Supply and Battery System

Two 11 kV station service transformers feed battery chargers that produce 220 V DC
and 48 V DC supplies:

- **220 V DC bank:** 110 batteries × 2 V, 300 Ah — powers protection relays, breaker
  trip/close circuits, and control panels
- **48 V DC bank:** 24 batteries × 2 V, 400 Ah — powers communication equipment and
  low-voltage DC loads

These ensure uninterrupted operation of protection and control systems during AC
supply failures.

## Relay Room and SCADA

Numerical (microprocessor-based) relays receive CT/CVT signals and trip circuit
breakers on fault detection. ANSI relay functions observed include:

- 21 – Distance Protection
- 25 – Synchronism Check
- 27 – Under Voltage
- 50 – Instantaneous Overcurrent
- 51 – Time Overcurrent
- 59 – Over Voltage
- 86 – Lockout Relay
- 87 / 87T – Differential / Transformer Differential Protection

The SCADA system enables remote monitoring and control of circuit breakers and
isolators, real-time voltage/current values, power flow, and alarm/fault indications,
working alongside PLCs to improve automation.

## Key Learning and Conclusion

The training provided practical exposure to switchyard layout, transformer cooling
and maintenance, auxiliary/battery power systems, numerical relay protection, and
SCADA-based control — connecting classroom power-systems theory to real-world
substation operation.
